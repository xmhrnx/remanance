Bring your files back to life.

DataDoc is free, open-source data recovery. It gets back files that were deleted, recovers drives that were formatted, and finds partitions that were lost. It reads the drive directly, through its own parsers for each file system, and never writes to it.

What it recovers

From a deleted file to a wiped partition table.

Scan a whole drive, one partition on it, or only one folder. A partition or a folder is quicker, since only its file system is read; files found by their signature need the whole drive.

Deleted files, with their names

Names, folders and dates come back from the file system's own records, read by DataDoc's own parsers.

NTFSFAT12/16/32exFAText2/3/4
  • NTFS: MFT records, fragmented files included, from their data runs.
  • ext4: inodes plus the names the journal still holds, often the only place they survive since Linux 5.13.

Lost partitions

A deep scan looks for file system boot sectors and superblocks across the drive, reading MBR and GPT tables on the way.

Failing drives

Copy a drive to an image once, then recover from the image as often as you like.

Deleted messages and records

Deleted rows in the SQLite databases apps keep: texts, call logs, contacts, notes and chats, saved as spreadsheets (CSV).

A health check for every file

DataDoc checks whether a file's clusters were reused since it was deleted, so you know what will open before you recover it.

Beside recovery

Four tools for the drive you still have.

In the rail on the left of the app, or the tab bar on a phone.

Disk space

Measures a drive or folder and draws it as a treemap, colored by kind of file.

Clean up

Finds temporary files, caches apps rebuild, crash reports, logs and the trash. Old downloads are listed unchecked, to look through first.

Duplicates

Exact copies by their contents, and with "Similar" on, photos that look the same and videos of the same clip.

Rename

Many files at once from a pattern such as {date} {n:3}, showing every new name and clash first, with undo.

Phones

Honest about what a phone gives back.

A phone encrypts its storage file by file and throws away a deleted file's key, so its sectors can't give the file back, with or without root. Here is what DataDoc can still find.

Android

  • Everything in the Android trash, from every app, for its 30 days.
  • Thumbnails and the copies apps hide behind .nomedia.
  • USB drives and memory cards on an OTG adapter, no root needed.
  • On rooted phones, deleted texts and chats in apps' databases.

iPhones and Android phones, from a computer

  • iPhones by USB: Recently Deleted, previews of deleted photos, and deleted rows in the Photos library.
  • Backups Finder or iTunes made, encrypted ones with their password.
  • Android phones by USB debugging, through ADB.

On iOS itself DataDoc recovers from disk images only, since iOS gives no app raw access to storage. Connect the iPhone to a computer and recover from there instead.

Command line

Everything the app does, for scripts and servers.

datadoc-cli lists drives and phones, scans, recovers, images failing drives, and runs every tool. rename only shows the new names until you add -yes.

$ datadoc-cli list
$ datadoc-cli scan -deep /dev/sdb
$ datadoc-cli recover -to ~/Recovered -type jpg,mp4 /dev/sdb
$ datadoc-cli image /dev/sdb sdb.img
$ datadoc-cli dupes -similar ~/Pictures
# also: space, clean, rename

Privacy

Your files stay on your devices.

This is DataDoc's privacy policy, for the app on every system and in every store. Last updated November 19, 2026.

Nothing is collected

  • No account, no analytics, no ads, no crash reports, no tracking.
  • DataDoc never connects to the internet. It sends nothing to its developer or anyone else.

What it reads

  • Only the drives, disk images, folders, backups and phones you choose to scan, measure or clean up.
  • Deleted messages, contacts and other records it finds are shown on your screen and saved only where you recover them.

What it writes

  • Recovered files, spreadsheets and disk images go to the folder you pick: on Android Download/DataDoc, on iOS the app's folder in Files.
  • It keeps no settings or history. A backup password lives in memory until you quit.

Phones and permissions. A phone connected to a computer talks to DataDoc over the USB cable, through Apple's own service for iPhones or Android's debug bridge (ADB) on this computer. When an iPhone is paired, the pairing record is kept by that service, as for Finder or iTunes. On Android, "All files access" lets DataDoc look through the phone's storage, and the USB permission lets it read a drive on an OTG adapter; the notification only shows a scan running in the background. Deleting files in Clean up, Disk space or Duplicates, and renaming in Rename, happen only when you confirm them. Questions: open an issue on GitHub.
